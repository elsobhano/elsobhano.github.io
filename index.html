I am a PhD researcher at the Centre for Vision, Speech and Signal Processing (CVSSP), University of Surrey, supervised by Prof. Richard Bowden. My thesis is on sign language recognition and translation.

My research focuses on gloss-free sign language translation: learning representations of sign directly from video, transferring motion-level knowledge across sign languages, and building models that do not depend on costly gloss annotation. I also work on sign dictionary retrieval and transferable sign representations. My work has appeared at ECCV, BMVC, ACM IVA and in CVIU.

Before Surrey, I completed an MSc in Electrical Engineering at Sharif University of Technology, where I worked on face forgery detection with Prof. Shahrokh Ghaemmaghami and Prof. Sajjad Amini, and a BSc in Electrical Engineering at Amirkabir University of Technology. I was also a research assistant at Sharif’s Electronic Research Institute, working on machine learning for speech processing.

selected publications

  1. Preprint
    SignSeek: Learning Transferable Representations for Sign Dictionary Retrieval
    Sobhan Asasi, Ozge Mercanoglu Sincan, and Richard Bowden
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.03695, Sep 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Automated sign language dictionary retrieval aims to find a sign in a dictionary given a query video, a task made difficult by the wide variability across signers. SignSeek employs contrastive learning with Articulator Saliency-Guided Masking (ASGM), which identifies the most critical articulator for each sign, and combines masked contrastive alignment with masked prediction losses. Trained on approximately 266,000 samples across multiple sign languages, the method achieves state-of-the-art performance on cross-corpus retrieval benchmarks (ASL-Citizen, WLASL, NMFs-CSL) without fine-tuning, and demonstrates zero-shot generalisation to unseen British Sign Language.

  2. ECCV
    SIGNET: Motion-Level Knowledge Transfer for Cross-Language Sign Language Translation
    Sobhan Asasi, Ozge Mercanoglu Sincan, and Richard Bowden
    In European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), Jun 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib Website

    Pretrained sign language models capture visual patterns that are reusable across datasets and languages. SIGNET integrates multiple pretrained sign language models through an attention-based, hand-prior aggregation mechanism combined with a gated fusion network, enabling motion-level knowledge transfer for cross-language sign language translation. Experiments on four benchmarks (How2Sign, Phoenix14T, CSL-Daily and MeineDGS) demonstrate state-of-the-art results, with additional gains on WLASL for sign language recognition.

  3. CVIU
    Gloss-free sign language translation: An unbiased evaluation of progress in the field
    Ozge Mercanoglu Sincan, Jian He Low, Sobhan Asasi, and 1 more author
    Computer Vision and Image Understanding (CVIU), Jun 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib HTML Code

    Sign Language Translation (SLT) systems convert sign language videos into spoken language text. We re-implement recent SLT models and evaluate them under standardised, consistent conditions. We find that many of the performance gains reported in the literature often diminish when models are evaluated under consistent conditions, offering an unbiased view of progress in the field.

  4. BMVC
    Beyond Gloss: A Hand-Centric Framework for Gloss-Free Sign Language Translation
    Sobhan Asasi, Mohamed Ilyes Lakhal, Ozge Mercanoglu Sincan, and 1 more author
    In British Machine Vision Conference (BMVC), Jul 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML Code

    BeyondGloss is a gloss-free sign language translation framework that leverages the spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities of Video Large Language Models (VideoLLMs). Since existing VideoLLMs struggle with long videos, we propose a novel approach to generate fine-grained, temporally-aware textual descriptions of hand motion, and introduce a contrastive alignment module that aligns these descriptions with video features during pre-training.

  5. ACM IVA
    Hierarchical Feature Alignment for Gloss-Free Sign Language Translation
    Sobhan Asasi, Mohamed Ilyes Lakhal, and Richard Bowden
    In ACM International Conference on Intelligent Virtual Agents (IVA), SLTAT Workshop, Jul 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib

    We propose a novel hierarchical pre-training strategy for sign language translation, inspired by the structure of sign language and incorporating pseudo-glosses and contrastive video-language alignment. The approach operates across frame, segment and video levels, aligning features with pseudo-glosses and target sentences, and yields measured improvements in BLEU-4 and ROUGE.
